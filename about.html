The Beginning

Thirty Years in the Room

There is a kind of hospitality that you feel the moment you walk into a room. The table is set with intention. The air smells like something worth sitting down for. The person who greets you makes you feel like you were genuinely expected. That quality, the thing that separates a meal from a memory, is what Charlie Accivatti has spent thirty years learning how to build.

He did not grow up wanting to be a chef. He grew up watching what happened at tables. Who relaxed. Who opened up. What food did to a conversation, to a mood, to a relationship. That observation became an education, and that education became a career spanning more than three decades and nearly every role the hospitality industry offers: line cook, executive chef, food and beverage director, managing director, restaurant consultant, company founder.

Early in his career, Charlie made a deliberate choice to move across disciplines rather than straight up one ladder. He wanted to understand what made an operation work at every level, how food costs affected menu design, how service culture shaped the guest experience, how a great dish could be undermined by a poorly run room. He spent years collecting those lessons in kitchens and dining rooms across the country, absorbing what worked and what did not.

By the time he was leading restaurant openings, he could read a room the way a conductor reads an orchestra. He knew what the kitchen needed to hear, what the floor staff needed to feel, and what a guest needed to believe in order to leave wanting to come back. That instinct, sharpened over thirty years, is what Charlie brings to every engagement today.

Career Scope

Opening Restaurants Across North America and Beyond

Restaurant openings are among the most demanding projects in hospitality. They require simultaneous management of construction timelines, menu development, staff recruitment and training, vendor relationships, and brand positioning, all converging on a single date that cannot move. Charlie has navigated that pressure multiple times, leading openings in markets as different from one another as Coral Gables, Dallas, Irving, Mexico City, and Fez.

Each market taught him something distinct. Coral Gables introduced him to a dining scene where international guests set a high floor for service expectations and attention to detail. Dallas and Irving gave him experience scaling hospitality for large-format events without losing the personal touch that makes a guest feel seen. Mexico City showed him how food culture and heritage can shape every dimension of a guest experience, from the sourcing of ingredients to the rhythm of service. And Fez, where hospitality is not a profession but a centuries-old philosophy, changed how he thought about what it means to welcome someone to a table.

He brought all of it back with him.

Companies & Roles

What He Built Along the Way

Founder · Sold 2008

Smart Catering

Charlie founded Smart Catering to close a gap he had seen throughout his career: operations producing good food on systems too inefficient to sustain quality at scale. Smart Catering was built from the ground up around operational infrastructure as much as culinary craft. It grew into a respected operation and was sold in 2008. The conviction behind it, that great hospitality requires great operations, runs through everything he has built since.

Managing Director

appétit

As Managing Director of appétit, a SaaS food delivery platform, Charlie brought his operational expertise to the intersection of hospitality and technology, two industries that often speak past each other. The role put him inside questions of systems design, user experience, and scalable service that most chefs never encounter. It reinforced a belief he has always held: the warmth a guest experiences at the table is the result of a thousand decisions made before they arrived.

Co-Owner · Currently Operating

Nur Restaurant, Fez

Nur Restaurant in Fez, Morocco is among the most meaningful chapters of Charlie's career, and the one still being written. Operating in one of the oldest medinas in the world, Nur takes Moroccan hospitality seriously as a living practice, bringing it to a dining experience built for the modern traveler and the regular local alike. Running a restaurant there requires a different kind of humility than opening a concept in a North American market. You are not introducing something new. You are earning a place in something much older.

Thought Leadership

Harvard. The World Science Festival. The Practical Meets the Scientific.

Not many people can say they have presented on food science at Harvard and at the World Science Festival. Charlie can. As a guest presenter at Harvard's Science of Cooking program and at the World Science Festival, he has engaged with audiences at the highest levels of academic and scientific inquiry, bringing the practitioner's perspective to conversations usually dominated by researchers.

These were not honorary appearances. They were substantive contributions to public conversations about how cooking works at a molecular and chemical level, and what that understanding means for the way professional chefs approach their craft.

For Charlie, science and cooking have always been the same discipline. Both are about understanding cause and effect well enough to predict outcomes and create something repeatable, reliably excellent. That rigor applies equally to a restaurant opening and to a pasta workshop in Buffalo, where participants learn not just how to make pasta but why it works, why resting the dough matters, why the finished texture reflects decisions made long before the pot hits the water. Understanding the why is what turns a recipe into a skill.

Western New York

Coming Home to Buffalo

Buffalo has always been home. After decades of working in major markets and international cities, Charlie returned to Western New York with something most chefs and hospitality professionals in the region simply do not have: a genuine international frame of reference and the operational depth to match it.

That perspective reshapes what private dining, catering, and hospitality events can look like here. The guests Charlie serves do not need to travel to experience a dinner with the precision of a great New York City restaurant or the warmth of a thoughtfully run operation in Fez. They can have it at their home, at their event, at their table, in Buffalo.

Western New York has always had the appetite for this. What it needed was someone who had seen what the best looked like elsewhere and cared enough to bring it back.

Today

Crystalline Management

Crystalline Management is Charlie Accivatti's private hospitality company, based in Buffalo, New York. It serves clients across Western New York and travels for events that call for it.

The work ranges from intimate private dinners for eight to full-room weddings, from wood-fired pop-up pizza events to pasta workshops where the goal is to teach something that lasts longer than the evening. Every engagement is designed from the ground up around the specific occasion. No set packages. No catering-hall shortcuts. Menus are built around the event, the season, and the people being fed.

What Crystalline Management offers is access to thirty years of professional hospitality experience, applied personally and with full attention, to whatever you are gathering people for. That is not a marketing claim. It is simply what thirty years of doing this looks like when it is pointed at your table.

Philosophy

On Mentorship and the People Behind the Food

Charlie is quick to acknowledge the people who shaped his career. The cooks who taught him knife skills before he was trusted with anything else. The general managers who showed him that a dining room is a living thing, responsive to mood and energy. The chefs in Morocco and Mexico who extended their trust and let him watch how they worked.

Mentorship runs in both directions in his operation. The team at Crystalline Management is trained not just on technique but on the philosophy behind it: why presentation matters, why timing matters, why the guest's experience from arrival to departure is a continuous conversation that the hospitality team is either improving or degrading. There is no neutral.

That conviction, that hospitality is something you do actively and not passively, is the thread connecting everything Charlie has built over thirty years. It is what brings him back to the kitchen for a private dinner in Orchard Park with the same energy he brought to a restaurant opening in Mexico City.

The table is always worth setting well.

— Charlie Accivatti
