Opening a boutique hotel restaurant

Crystalline led the opening effort for a boutique hotel’s new restaurant. Charlie brought ownership and hotel leadership together with culinary, beverage, service, design, marketing, accounting and HR. He connected decisions that crossed departments: how the dining room would work in service, what the opening menu required from purchasing and staffing, and what the budget could support at each phase. He made clear who owned each decision, held teams accountable and guided them through opening service.

Crystalline Hospitality DNA

Charlie trained the team to notice what a guest needed, take ownership of a request and follow through. When a guest asked how they were doing, the answer was “I’m happy.” When a guest made a respectful request the team could fulfill, it was “It’s Our Pleasure.” Charlie encouraged each person to let their individuality shine. He reinforced the standard through live coaching and gave managers an eight-module hospitality curriculum to carry it forward.

How I work

In the building, with your team.

I’ve worked every side of a hospitality business: line cook, floor manager, food and beverage director, catering company founder, restaurant owner. That’s why I see a dining room, a purchasing sheet and a staff schedule as one problem.

I’ve run operations in Dallas, Miami, New York, Mexico, Morocco, Australia, Lebanon, China and Puerto Rico. Each market works differently, and I’ve learned to adapt the standard to the people delivering it.

On an engagement I bring ownership, leadership and department heads around one plan and make clear who owns each decision. Then I’m on the floor beside them in service, where a plan either works or it doesn’t.

  1. Look at the whole house

    Time with ownership, leadership and the team, on the floor and in the numbers, before I recommend anything.

  2. Agree on one plan

    Every department sees how its decisions land on the others, and every decision has a named owner.

  3. Lead through it

    Live coaching in service, and I stay through the moments that matter most, like opening night.
