Making Operations Visible

FieldLoop is an operational intelligence initiative exploring how daily operational activity can be organized into meaningful insight.

Operators already collect an enormous amount of information. Schedules, checklists, vendor deliveries, maintenance requests, shift notes, inventory counts. Each one carries a signal. The challenge is seeing what those signals reveal when they are viewed together.

The Problem

The Problem Is Not Lack of Activity

Walk into any well-run operation and you will find systems everywhere. A POS full of transactions. A schedule that took hours to build. Checklists signed off every night. Invoices from a dozen vendors. Text threads where the real problems get solved at six in the morning.

None of that is the problem. The problem is that each system only sees itself. The walk-in that fails every August lives in the maintenance log. The prep list that falls apart whenever one particular cook is out lives in a manager's head. The vendor whose short deliveries quietly cost a point of food cost lives in a stack of invoices nobody reconciles against the order.

Individually these are annoyances. Connected, they are a picture of where the operation actually leaks time and money. Most operators never get to see that picture.

Origin

Why FieldLoop Exists

Charlie Accivatti has spent more than thirty years inside operations. Opening restaurants. Running high-volume catering in New York. Working the kitchen and the dining room in the same shift. Building a food program in a small-town wine bar. Teaching the science of cooking at Harvard.

Across all of it, one pattern kept repeating: the expensive problems almost never announced themselves. They arrived as small things. A closing standard that slipped on the same night every week. A handoff between shifts that kept dropping the same task. A repair that got patched four times before anyone noticed it was the same repair. A supplier shorting the same item every other order.

The idea behind FieldLoop is simple. The information that predicts those problems already exists inside the operation. What is missing is a way to see it early, with names and dates attached.

FieldLoop is technology in service of that insight. Not the other way around.

Scope

What FieldLoop Is Exploring

FieldLoop looks at the signals an operation already produces and asks how they relate to one another.

  • Opening and closing standards
  • Prep execution
  • Labor activity
  • Inventory movement
  • Vendor reliability
  • Maintenance activity
  • Event execution
  • Manager accountability
  • Performance across locations

No single category says much on its own. The relationships between them are where the picture forms.

Questions

Questions Worth Asking

The clearest way to describe FieldLoop is not a feature list. It is a set of questions.

  • Which recurring issues create the most downstream disruption?
  • Which managers consistently reduce friction?
  • Which locations require disproportionate oversight?
  • Which vendors generate hidden costs?
  • Which maintenance issues keep affecting service quality?
  • Which labor patterns correlate with turnover?
  • Which problems stay invisible until someone connects the dots?

If one of these made you think of something specific in your own operation, that is exactly the kind of conversation FieldLoop is built on.

Get Involved

Built With Operators, Not For Operators

FieldLoop is being shaped through direct conversations with the people who run things. Restaurant owners, general managers, catering operators, club executives, hospitality leaders of every kind. Especially anyone who has ever inherited a problem that turned out to be six months old.
